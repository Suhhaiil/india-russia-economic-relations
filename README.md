# India–Russia Economic Relations: A Data-Driven Analysis of Bilateral Trade, 2014–2025

## Overview

This independent research and data analytics project examines the evolution of India–Russia bilateral merchandise trade from 2014–15 to 2024–25.

The project analyses changes in exports, imports, total trade, trade balance, import dependence and year-on-year trade growth, with particular attention to the major transformation in bilateral trade after 2021–22.

## Research Question

How have India–Russia bilateral economic relations evolved between 2014–15 and 2024–25, and what does the changing structure of bilateral trade reveal about India's trade dependence on Russia?

## Key Findings

- Bilateral trade increased from approximately **US$6.35 billion** in 2014–15 to **US$68.69 billion** in 2024–25.
- Total trade recorded its strongest year-on-year growth in **2022–23 at 276.08%**.
- Imports from Russia increased much more rapidly than Indian exports to Russia.
- Imports accounted for an average of approximately **77.60%** of bilateral trade during the study period.
- India's bilateral trade deficit with Russia reached approximately **US$58.93 billion in 2024–25**.
- The post-2022 expansion was strongly import-driven, highlighting questions around trade concentration, import dependence and export diversification.

## Methodology

The project follows a data-driven descriptive research approach using:

- **Microsoft Excel** — data organisation, calculations, quality checks, PivotTable and initial visualisations
- **Python (pandas and matplotlib)** — descriptive analysis and data visualisation
- **Power BI** — interactive dashboard and visual analysis

### Data Source

The primary quantitative dataset was compiled from the **Government of India, Department of Commerce TradeStat system**, covering financial years 2014–15 to 2024–25.

Additional contextual sources include the Ministry of External Affairs, Government of India, and the World Bank's World Integrated Trade Solution (WITS).

## Limitations

The analysis focuses on bilateral merchandise trade and does not quantitatively cover services, foreign direct investment or the full range of India–Russia economic cooperation.

The analysis is descriptive rather than causal and therefore does not establish direct causal relationships between geopolitical developments and trade outcomes.
